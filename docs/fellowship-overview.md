# Fellowship Overview

## Fellowship Objectives

The fellowship aims to produce a technically grounded contribution to AI
standardisation for trustworthy machine learning systems in financial
services. The work is intended to bridge standards activity and real-world
implementation by showing how governance, lifecycle discipline, reliability
assessment, and evidence management can be made operational.

The primary objectives are to:

- interpret relevant AI standards for financial-sector implementation
- identify areas where current standards are mature and where they are not
- surface practical control patterns for trustworthy AI deployment
- document gaps that matter for advanced ML, LLM, and agentic systems
- provide recommendations that can inform future standardisation work

## Scope of Work

The repository focuses on standards-adjacent technical analysis rather than
normative reproduction. The scope includes:

- AI management, governance, and accountability structures
- AI risk management practices and control integration
- lifecycle processes for model development, deployment, and retirement
- reliability assessment approaches for production AI systems
- financial-sector implementation issues such as drift, auditability, and
  incident handling
- evidence capture needed for public reporting and ongoing standards
  engagement

It excludes reproducing proprietary standards clauses or redistributing
copyrighted materials.

## AI Standardisation Landscape

The current landscape is fragmented across governance, risk, lifecycle,
quality, and reliability perspectives. No single standard presently provides
complete operational coverage for all financial AI use cases. A practical
implementation program therefore needs a layered view:

- management system standards define roles, controls, and accountability
- risk standards frame hazard identification and treatment planning
- lifecycle standards structure engineering activities and handoffs
- reliability and quality initiatives support measurable system assurance
- sustainability initiatives shape resource and operational design choices

This layered model is especially important in finance, where model portfolios,
regulatory expectations, and control evidence must work together.

## Expected Outcomes

Expected public outputs from the fellowship include:

- a reusable standards landscape for trustworthy AI in finance
- documented mappings from standards themes to operational controls
- gap analyses for under-specified areas such as adaptive and agentic systems
- recommendations for future standardisation priorities
- structured notes and evidence that can be cited in the final fellowship
  report and related dissemination materials

## Standards Engagement Approach

The engagement approach is designed to be disciplined and evidence-led:

1. identify the publicly visible purpose and scope of a standard or initiative
2. extract the implementation questions most relevant to financial services
3. compare the standard's focus with production control needs
4. record strengths, omissions, and overlap with adjacent work
5. turn observations into recommendations for standards participants and
   practitioners

This method supports both standards engagement and practical industry use.

## Public Dissemination Approach

The repository is intended to function as a single public artifact rather than
splitting outputs across disconnected documents. That allows future standards
reviews, meeting notes, standards-body engagement observations, blog drafts,
and reporting evidence to accumulate in one traceable location.

Public dissemination should therefore prioritize:

- concise, technically credible markdown documents
- explicit separation between source description and original analysis
- traceable links from claims to stored evidence
- regular updates that show progression of the fellowship work
- reusability of material in presentations, reports, and open technical
  discussion
